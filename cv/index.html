I take geopolitical events, financial disclosure and incomplete evidence and turn them into stated judgements: probability and confidence given separately, indicators with thresholds set before the data is consulted, and the conditions that would prove the judgement wrong written down alongside it. Claims are labelled fact, calculation or assessment, and questions the evidence does not settle are recorded as unsettled rather than filled.

The work spans geopolitical and security risk, sanctions and corporate ownership, credit and financial-crime exposure, and maritime and aviation verification. Intelligence practice and method

Tradecraft

Intelligence cycle and requirements. PHIA probability yardstick, with confidence stated separately from probability. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses and dimension-specific confidence grading. Source grading and reliability. Indicators and warning. Falsifiers and model correction. National Intelligence Model product taxonomy. Explicit recording of intelligence gaps rather than inference.

Products

Strategic assessments, problem profiles, target profiles, SITREPs and single-page decision briefs. Assessments presented and defended before an intelligence committee on the KSG track. Escalation reporting to senior leadership under time pressure.

Evidential discipline

Every figure traced to a primary filing or registry, with a standing rule that a search-engine summary is a lead and never a source. Applied in practice: that rule has caught errors in my own finished drafts, including an untraced statistic, and the corrections were published rather than quietly made.

AI in open-source work

Used for collection triage and drafting inside that verification discipline, with human source checking and evidential judgement retained. King's College London, War Studies: Artificial Intelligence in National Security. I do not claim experience building or deploying AI tooling.
