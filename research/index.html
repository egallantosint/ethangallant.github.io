Commercial Infrastructure, Strategic Dependence

Mediterranean container terminals July 2026

A forty page open source study mapping Chinese state-linked ownership across eleven Mediterranean container terminals and assessing each position against NATO logistical exposure. Confidence is graded separately for commercial influence, military utility and evidence of operational use, because the evidence behind each is qualitatively different.

The finding that carries: dependence operates at the level of the corporate network, not the individual port. When the Red Sea closed in 2024 and Piraeus throughput fell, the traffic reappeared at Valencia and Zeebrugge, and never left the COSCO perimeter.

Two positions where beneficial ownership could not be established from open sources are recorded as intelligence gaps rather than inferred.

How I work

Confidence, stated per claim

A judgement graded low next to one graded high is more use than a document with a single confidence line at the top. Grading claim by claim also forces the weak ones into the open, where they can be argued with.

Gaps recorded, not filled

Where the open record does not answer a question, the question is written down along with where it was looked for. Substituting a plausible figure destroys the distinction between what is known and what is assumed, which is the only thing making the rest of the work checkable.
